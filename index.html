Abstract

We tackle the problem of sparse novel view synthesis (NVS) using video diffusion models; given K (≈5) posed images of a scene, we predict the view from a target camera pose. Many prior approaches leverage generative image priors encoded via diffusion models. However, models trained on single images lack multi-view knowledge, and we instead argue that video models contain implicit multi-view knowledge and are therefore easier to adapt for NVS. Our key insight is to formulate sparse NVS as a low-frame-rate video completion task. However, one challenge is that sparse NVS is defined over an unordered set of input images, often too sparse to admit a meaningful order, so the models should be invariant to permutations of that input image set.

To this end, we present FrameCrafter, which adapts video models (naturally trained with coherent frame orderings) to permutation-invariant NVS through simple but effective architectural modifications, including per-frame latent encoding, query-centered camera conditioning and removal of temporal positional embeddings. Our results suggest that video models can be easily modified to “forget” about time with minimal supervision, producing state-of-the-art performance on sparse-view NVS benchmarks.

01 · The idea

Novel view synthesis is
low-frame-rate video completion

Scroll to see how a video model, trained to fill in missing frames of ordered clips, becomes a model that completes a missing view from an unordered set of posed photos.

STEP 1 / 5

Video models complete videos.

A video diffusion model learns to generate frames that stay consistent with the rest of a clip. To do this well, it has to know how a scene looks as the camera moves, so it has learned a lot of multi-view geometry along the way.

STEP 2 / 5

Sparse views are a very low-frame-rate video.

Replace the dense clip with K≈6 posed photos taken far apart, and ask for the frame at a new query camera. Sparse-view NVS now looks just like completing a missing frame.

STEP 3 / 5

But a set of photos has no order.

Sparse captures are often too far apart to admit a meaningful sequence. A video model indexes frames by time, so feeding the same photos in a different order hands each one a different time index and can change the answer.

STEP 4 / 5

So we make the model forget time.

Each view is encoded on its own, cameras are expressed relative to the query, and temporal positions are zeroed out. The views become an unordered set, tied together only by their camera rays.

STEP 5 / 5

…and complete the missing view.

The video DiT attends jointly over all views; the query latent is the only output we keep. Keep scrolling to run the denoising steps: the model's clean-image estimate goes from blurry layout to sharp detail.

02 · Method

Three simple changes turn a video model into a multi-view model

We start from Wan2.1-I2V-14B. Each input view is encoded by the frozen video VAE, concatenated with a Plücker ray map of its camera, and all view tokens (plus a zero latent for the query) are processed by the DiT. Only the patch embedding and LoRA modules are trained; only the query latent is decoded and supervised.

Each input image is encoded independently by a frozen video VAE and channel-concatenated with its Plücker ray map. View tokens are concatenated along the (former) temporal axis; the last token is the query. Click to enlarge.
1

Per-view VAE encoding

A causal video VAE mixes neighbouring frames and compresses time by 4×. We encode each view as its own 1-frame “video”, exactly as image-to-video models already treat the first frame.

Without per-view encoding, PSNR drops from 15.69 to 11.50: the single most important change.
2

Query-centered cameras

Defining the world frame by the first input ties every camera to input order. We put the origin at the query camera and scale inputs to unit mean distance.

origin:
With the query as origin, reordering the inputs leaves every coordinate unchanged.
3

No temporal positions

3D RoPE gives every token a time index. We zero out its temporal component, so attention can only tell views apart by their camera rays, not by their slot in the sequence.

Removing temporal RoPE also improves quality: PSNR 15.30 → 15.69, LPIPS 0.265 → 0.246.

What the model sees: image latents + Plücker ray maps

Each view carries a per-pixel Plücker ray map [d, o×d] (pixel-unshuffled to latent resolution, so no ray detail is lost). Because the world frame sits at the query camera, the query's moment map o×d is identically zero.

03 · Inside the sampler

Watch a novel view emerge

We recorded all 50 sampling steps of FrameCrafter for a held-out target view. Drag the slider or press play. Within the first step the model already knows the layout of the unseen view; the remaining steps add detail, which shows up as better LPIPS.

predicted final image vs. ground truth, per step
Predicted final image x̂₀. At each step the network looks at the current noisy sample and predicts the flow velocity v; from it we can read off its current guess of the finished image, x̂₀ = zt − σt·v. This is what the model “believes” the novel view looks like at that point.
Current noisy sample zt. The image the sampler is actually holding, a blend (1−σt)·x̂₀ + σt·noise. Wan's shifted schedule keeps the noise level σt high for most steps, so it looks like noise until roughly the last 15 steps.
Why PSNR goes down. Early guesses are blurry averages, which PSNR rewards. As sampling commits to sharp detail, LPIPS (perceptual error) improves while PSNR dips: the usual perception–distortion trade-off. Metrics use the paper's 480×480 center crop.

On the same CogVideoX-5B backbone, our recipe already improves over Aether on nearly every metric, and moving to a stronger video backbone helps across the board.

Even on the ordered model's home turf, permutation-invariant training wins. Sparse views rarely lie on a smooth path, and every reordering of a training sample is a valid new sample, a factorially larger training set for free.

Data efficiency: the prior comes from video

Trained on just 20 scenes, FrameCrafter already beats EscherNet trained on 10K scenes, 500× less data. Performance keeps rising with more scenes.

The lightweight training mostly teaches the model the sparse-view format and camera conditioning; the multi-view reasoning itself appears to have emerged during large-scale video pretraining. Compared to SEVA, training uses 7.8× fewer trainable parameters, 30× fewer iterations and 80× less multi-view data.

06 · Discussion

Do video world models know about time?

We were surprised by the ease with which such models can be tuned to be permutation-invariant, losing any sense of temporal dynamics. Current models might capture more “multi-view” knowledge about the world than the dynamics of how it evolves over time.

From the conclusion. At the very least, temporal knowledge is surprisingly easy to unlearn.

Practically, this points to a scalable recipe: instead of curating ever-larger posed multi-view datasets, treat multi-view synthesis as a lightweight specialization of video foundation models, and let gains in video pretraining flow directly into novel view synthesis.

What's next: encode the inputs once

Today every input view is re-processed by the DiT at every denoising step, so self-attention cost grows quadratically with the number of inputs. Encoding the inputs once and injecting them through cross-attention would make the per-step cost linear and let the model scale to many more views.
